# Channel 14 governs Atmos, not the audio

In the cinema, Atmos is not "selected" by the server: **the processor (CP850, CP950A, IMS3000) plays Atmos only while it can lock onto the sync signal on channel 14 (0-based index 13) of the main PCM sound MXF; if it loses lock, it automatically reverts ("reverted") to the 5.1/7.1 mix on channels 1-8**. Channel 14 is not audio, but an FSK data stream carrying the asset UUID and a frame counter. Only the processor consumes it, and listening to it in isolation serves no programme purpose. The Atmos essence is a separate SMPTE MXF (DCData with a `DolbyAtmosSubDescriptor`, or IAB per ST 429-18). Open tools (asdcplib, libdcp) wrap and unwrap it, but none renders Dolby's proprietary Atmos. The only open rendering path is IAB (ST 2098-2), via IABLib or easyDCP Player+. In DCP-o-matic, Atmos is a pure pass-through: `Player::Atmos` is only consumed by `DCPFilmEncoder`, the player never renders it, and libdcp generates the channel 14 FSK when writing the MXF if the film contains Atmos. Selecting channel 14 in real time is already possible today, indirectly, via the `AudioMapping` matrix in Preferences > Sound. Making that clean would be a small change. Playing back Atmos in real time would require a decoder and a renderer that do not exist in the project and that, for encrypted proprietary Atmos, are not feasible.

## Channel 14 is an ISDCF convention, not a SMPTE rule

The assignment comes from **ISDCF Doc 4** ("16-Channel Audio Packaging Guide", 2017). Numbering there is 1-based: 13 Motion Data (D-Box) and **14 Sync Signal, "Used for external sync (e.g. FSK Sync) - only used for SMPTE-DCP - NOT INTEROP-DCP"**. The document itself is a common-practice recommendation and says the ordering "commonly maps one-to-one to the audio outputs of a player". That is why the sync signal comes out of output 14 of the server or IMB ([ISDCF Doc 4](https://files.isdcf.com/papers/ISDCF-Doc4-Audio-channel-recommendations.pdf)). SMPTE ST 430-14:2015 defines a digital sync signal "mapped to a single AES3 channel", but does not fix any channel number ([SMPTE ST 430-14:2015](https://pub.smpte.org/doc/st430-14/20150801-pub/st0430-14-2015.pdf)). What does fix it is Dolby's documentation: the CP850 "uses a synchronization signal embedded into channel 14 of the DCP Main Soundtrack", carrying "the ID of the track and frame position" ([CP850 Release Notes v2.1.0.10](https://smart-story.ru/files/products/multimedia/Audio_processors/Dolby/CP850/Docs/dolby_cp850_release_notes_v2_1_0_10.pdf)). The CP950A manual requires that "the macro must have Dolby Atmos sync assigned to channel 14" ([CP950/CP950A Manual Issue 13](https://professional.dolby.com/siteassets/products/cp950a/dolby_cp950-cp950a_manual_issue_13.pdf)).

The "channel 13 or 14" confusion is purely a numbering-base issue. In libdcp, `dcp::Channel` has `MOTION_DATA=12` and `SYNC_SIGNAL=13`, 0-based, while the `MCAChannelID` written to the MXF is `dcp_channel + 1` ([libdcp types.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/types.h), [sound_asset_writer.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/sound_asset_writer.cc)). There are two practical consequences:

1. A DCP with fewer than 14 channels cannot carry a sync signal. That is why Atmos DCPs are made with 14 or 16 channels, with the 5.1/7.1 fallback on channels 1-6 or 1-8 ([DCP-o-matic forum t=1845](https://dcpomatic.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1845)).
2. Everything here is SMPTE-only: the sync signal (ISDCF, and libdcp's `standard == SMPTE` assert) and also the Atmos asset itself (`MXF (Standard::SMPTE)` in [libdcp atmos_asset.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/atmos_asset.cc)).

Two signal families are often confused under the name "channel 14 sync":

| | FSK (libdcp / asdcplib) | SMPTE ST 430-14 |
|---|---|---|
| Generated by | Baked into the MXF at mastering | Emitted live by the IMB during playback |
| Encoding | FSK, 4 samples/bit at 48 kHz, header `0x4D 0x56`, CRC-16 `0x1021` | 24-bit Lead/Tail pairs, marker `0xAAF0`, peaks ≈ −36 dBFS |
| Content | Edit-rate code, packet index, 4 bytes of UUID per packet, 24-bit frame counter | Status, edit-unit index, CPL and track-file UUIDs, offsets |
| Source | [libdcp sound_asset_writer.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/sound_asset_writer.cc), [fsk.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/fsk.cc) | [ST 430-14](https://pub.smpte.org/doc/st430-14/20150801-pub/st0430-14-2015.pdf) |

libdcp's FSK sends 4 packets per frame at 24/25/30 fps, 2 at 48/50/60 fps and 1 at 96/100/120 fps. At 24/25/30 fps, therefore, the whole UUID is transmitted every frame. There is no public Dolby specification for the FSK. The origin of libdcp's `0x4D56` format is undocumented, and Mantis issue #1777 describes it, oddly, "as per ST-430-12" ([Mantis 1777](https://www.dcpomatic.com/mantis/view.php?id=1777)). It is also unresolved who generates the signal on current Dolby hardware. One easyDCP guide states that track 14 is left silent and the sync signal is generated at playback ([knuterikevensen.com](https://www.knuterikevensen.com/2021/07/13/how-to-make-a-7-1-vf-dcp-in-easydcp-creator-plus/), search-snippet only). By contrast, libdcp, asdcplib (`asdcp-wrap -s`) and the easyDCP Player documentation bake it into the sound MXF ([easyDCP Audio Routing](https://docs.easydcp.com/products/player/audio_routing.html)). There is also an unresolved discrepancy over which UUID is encoded: in libdcp it is the **main sound MXF**'s UUID; in asdcplib, the mixer is built with the **Atmos track**'s UUID ([AtmosSyncChannel_Mixer.cpp](https://github.com/cinecert/asdcplib/blob/master/src/AtmosSyncChannel_Mixer.cpp)). Either way, the signal is tied to one specific asset. Re-wrapping or changing either MXF requires regenerating channel 14.

## Atmos travels in its own MXF, delivered to the processor ahead of the show

"Legacy" Atmos is a separate SMPTE track file, frame-wrapping D-Cinema data: `PrivateDCDataDescriptor` + `DolbyAtmosSubDescriptor` (AtmosVersion, MaxChannelCount, MaxObjectCount, AtmosID, FirstFrame) and DataEssenceCoding UL `06.0e.2b.34.04.01.01.05.0e.09.06.04.00.00.00.00` ([asdcplib AS_DCP_ATMOS.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/asdcplib/master/src/AS_DCP_ATMOS.cpp), [MDD.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/asdcplib/master/src/MDD.cpp)). Each frame corresponds to one picture edit unit. The CPL references it via `axd:AuxData` (namespace `http://www.dolby.com/schemas/2012/AD`) ([libdcp reel_atmos_asset.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/reel_atmos_asset.cc)). IAB is its standardized successor: an ST 2098-2 bitstream inside an ST 429-18 track file, with the DCP constraints of ST 429-19 and the ISDCF Doc 15 / RDD 57 Profile 1. Profile 1 allows at most 10 bed channels and 118 objects, at 48 kHz. Per Doc 15, "It is believed that legacy content (Dolby Atmos) follows the guidelines of IAB Profile 1" ([ISDCF Doc 15](https://files.isdcf.com/papers/ISDCF-Doc15-IAB-Profile-1-202006012.pdf)). The DataType UL is the same in both cases ([DCP Inside: IAB](https://sherpadown.net/dcp-inside/IAB.en)).

On encryption, Atmos/IAB essence uses the **MDEK** key type, and a DCP may need two KDMs: one for the media block and one for the immersive processor. Atmos content KDMs must carry the `disable-above-channel-12` flag. Without it, Audio Forensic Marking touches channels 13-16 and corrupts the sync signal — Dolby's first-cited cause of "reverted to PCM" ([CP850 Release Notes](https://smart-story.ru/files/products/multimedia/Audio_processors/Dolby/CP850/Docs/dolby_cp850_release_notes_v2_1_0_10.pdf), [CP950A Manual](https://professional.dolby.com/siteassets/products/cp950a/dolby_cp950-cp950a_manual_issue_13.pdf)). DCP Inside states that in an IAB DCP the MainSound track is otherwise empty except for accessibility and motion channels. This contradicts the common practice of Atmos with a full 5.1/7.1 fallback, and could not be verified against the ST 429-19 text.

## The processor, not the server, chooses in real time

Per the CP950A manual, Atmos tracks "are hosted on the playback system and transferred to the CP950A in advance of playback". During the show, the channel 14 signal "provides clip information and timing information to match the video and audio" ([CP950A Manual](https://professional.dolby.com/siteassets/products/cp950a/dolby_cp950-cp950a_manual_issue_13.pdf)). Atmos↔PCM switching is automatic and continuous. The main PCM always plays, and the Atmos render is layered on top only while the sync signal is valid. Documented controls:

1. **Status**: `pcm 5.1`, `pcm 7.1`, `atmos` and `reverted` (Atmos present but not processable, so PCM plays). The CP850 reports 20 ms latency in PCM and 80 ms in PCM + Atmos ([CP850 Release Notes](https://smart-story.ru/files/products/multimedia/Audio_processors/Dolby/CP850/Docs/dolby_cp850_release_notes_v2_1_0_10.pdf)).
2. **Operator**: macro/format selection (the "16ch MediaBlock + Dolby Atmos" input plays Atmos "when available") and an enable/disable Dolby Atmos switch in the CP950A's system configuration ([CP950A Manual](https://professional.dolby.com/siteassets/products/cp950a/dolby_cp950-cp950a_manual_issue_13.pdf)).
3. **Failure**: if the CAT1710 media block fails, the CP950A falls back to a failover room configuration, 5.1/7.1 only ([CP950A Manual](https://professional.dolby.com/siteassets/products/cp950a/dolby_cp950-cp950a_manual_issue_13.pdf)).
4. **Third-party server integration**: the Barco ICMP-X will not offer Atmos/IAB CPLs until the CP950A's "Server Compatibility Mode" is set to Barco ([Film-Tech](https://ft-forum.com/ft/vbb/forum/main-forum/49620-barco-icmp-x-with-cp950a-atmos-gotcha)).

No control was found for listening to channel 14 alone. In the macro it is flagged as the sync channel and is not routed to the room. No primary documentation was found for Christie, GDC or Doremi, nor for revert/re-lock timings.

## Outside the cinema, only IAB can be rendered

Third-party tool landscape:

1. **asdcplib** reads and writes Atmos (`ESS_DCDATA_DOLBY_ATMOS`) and IAB IMF (`ESS_AS02_IAB`). `asdcp-wrap -s` inserts the sync channel, using Channel Configuration 4, and `asdcp-unwrap` extracts `.atmos` frames. It renders nothing ([AS_DCP.h](https://github.com/cinecert/asdcplib/blob/master/src/AS_DCP.h), [asdcp-wrap.cpp](https://github.com/cinecert/asdcplib/blob/master/src/asdcp-wrap.cpp), [asdcp-unwrap.cpp](https://github.com/cinecert/asdcplib/blob/master/src/asdcp-unwrap.cpp)).
2. **easyDCP Player+ ≥ 4.3** plays back DCP IAB, "including Dolby Atmos content", to stereo, 5.1, 7.1 or 7.1.4 if "IAB Auxiliary Track" is chosen over the channel mix. It also has per-channel input/output routing ([easyDCP FAQ](https://en.easydcp.com/support-faq.php?id=179&p=playing-back-a-dcp-with-immersive-audio-bitstream-iab-dolby-atmos-with-easydcp-player-plus), [Audio Routing](https://docs.easydcp.com/products/player/audio_routing.html)). It is undocumented whether IAB↔PCM switching can be done live, or whether non-IAB legacy Atmos renders.
3. **IABLib** (DTSProAudio/iab-renderer) is an open C++ library that creates, parses and renders IAB ST 2098-2, with a file-based (offline) rendering app ([iab-renderer](https://github.com/DTSProAudio/iab-renderer)). A Profile 1 validator ships alongside it ([iab-validator](https://github.com/DTSProAudio/iab-validator)). Since legacy Atmos is believed to be Profile 1, IABLib might in principle read unencrypted Atmos frames unwrapped with asdcplib — unverified.
4. **Creation**: mastering a theatrical Atmos MXF requires a Dolby theatrical RMU. The IAB IMF produced by the Production/Mastering Suite "is not compatible with DCP" ([Dolby developer KB](https://developerkb.dolby.com/support/discussions/topics/16000027539)). CineIA_CLI converts IAB IMF (ST 2067-201) to IAB DCP ([CineIA_CLI](https://github.com/izwb003/CineIA_CLI)).
5. **Isolated channel 14**: it is ordinary 24-bit PCM inside the sound MXF. Any MXF-reading tool can extract it (e.g. ffmpeg with `pan`/`channelmap`) — an inference, not checked against a primary source.

No open renderer exists for Dolby's proprietary Atmos, and encrypted Atmos requires the MDEK key from a KDM targeted at a certificate the tool holds. No open real-time IAB player was found either.

## DCP-o-matic re-wraps Atmos and writes the sync signal, but never plays it back

The entire local-repo Atmos chain is a pass-through of opaque `dcp::AtmosFrame` blobs:

1. **Input**: `AtmosMXFContent::valid_mxf()` detects an Atmos MXF by attempting to construct a `dcp::AtmosAsset` (`src/lib/atmos_mxf_content.cc:56-73`). `AtmosMXFDecoder` reads one frame per video frame with `set_check_hmac(false)` (`src/lib/atmos_mxf_decoder.cc:35-69`). From a DCP, `DCPDecoder` creates an `AtmosDecoder` (`src/lib/dcp_decoder.cc:93-94`).
2. **Player**: `Player::atmos()` converts the time to DCP time and emits the `Atmos` signal (`src/lib/player.cc:1636-1664`). The **only** `Atmos.connect` in the tree is `src/lib/dcp_film_encoder.cc:69`. `Butler` and `FilmViewer` never receive it, so Atmos frames are dropped during preview and playback.
3. **Output**: `ReelWriter::write` builds a new `dcp::AtmosAsset` via `AtmosMetadata::create` (i.e. a new UUID) and copies the payload into it (`src/lib/reel_writer.cc:308-319`, `751-752`).
4. **Film rules**: adding Atmos content forces `audio_channels >= 14` and `set_interop(false)` (`src/lib/film.cc:1558-1583`), forces the film's edit rate to match the Atmos content's (`film.cc:1787-1803`), and appends "-IAB" to the ISDCF name (`film.cc:1127-1129`).
5. **Sync signal**: `src/lib/reel_writer.cc:239` passes `film()->contains_atmos_content() ? dcp::SoundAsset::AtmosSync::ENABLED : ...DISABLED`. When enabled, libdcp's `SoundAssetWriter` replaces channel `j == 13` with `_fsk.get()`, discarding whatever audio was mapped there ([sound_asset_writer.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cth103/libdcp/main/src/sound_asset_writer.h)). This implements Mantis issue #1777, resolved in 2.16.0 ([Mantis 1777](https://www.dcpomatic.com/mantis/view.php?id=1777)). DCP-o-matic treats index 13 as a pass-through channel in audio processors (`src/lib/audio_processor.cc:151-161`) and weights it at −144 dB in the LEQ(m) analysis (`src/lib/audio_analyser.cc:114`, `// Sync`).

There is a labelling inconsistency. `short_audio_channel_name` calls index 13 "DBS" and `audio_channel_name` calls it "D-BOX secondary" (`src/lib/util.cc:567-620`), while libdcp calls it `SYNC_SIGNAL`. `AudioProcessor::input_names()` also shifts "DBP"/"DBS" one position relative to `util.cc` (`src/lib/audio_processor.cc:139-148`). In the mapping grid, therefore, the channel libdcp overwrites with FSK shows up labelled "DBS".

As for selecting channel 14 in real time in the player, the path is `Player` → `Butler` (`remap(audio, _audio_channels, _audio_mapping)`, `src/lib/butler.cc:376`) → the RtAudio callback (`src/wx/film_viewer.cc:731-758`). The standalone player forces 16 channels (`src/tools/dcpomatic_player.cc:501`). By default, the `Config::audio_mapping` matrix does an Lt/Rt downmix on stereo output, which excludes index 13, and is 1:1 when there are 14 or more outputs (`src/lib/config.cc:1519-1545`). There is no mute or solo control. Channel 14 can be isolated today by editing the matrix in Preferences > Sound (`src/wx/sound_preferences_page.cc`). `FilmViewer::config_changed(Config::AUDIO_MAPPING)` rebuilds the `Butler` and re-seeks (`src/wx/film_viewer.cc:600-604`), so it works during playback, but with a glitch. The change is also global and persistent. It is only meaningful with a DCP that already carries FSK — in the editor, the FSK does not exist yet, since it is synthesized by libdcp only when the MXF is written.

A clean channel 14 solo would only need a player control that builds an `AudioMapping(16, outputs)` with just input 13 routed to the outputs, passed to the `Butler`. The variant without a rebuild would be a mutex-protected `Butler::set_audio_mapping()`. That variant has a limit: audio already queued was remapped with the old matrix, so the change would arrive after a buffer's worth of delay. Playing back Atmos, by contrast, would need three new pieces:

1. A subscriber to `Player::Atmos` on the `Butler`/`FilmViewer` path.
2. An IAB decoder and renderer synchronized to `DCPTime`.
3. Mixing or replacing the bed.

No current dependency covers this, and encrypted or proprietary Atmos would remain out of reach regardless. No forum or Mantis request was found for either feature. As a side note, the forum has January 2026 reports of a 7-reel Atmos DCP where only the first reel was incorporated correctly; not checked against the code ([forum t=2854](https://dcpomatic.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=2854)).

## Conclusion

"Selecting track 14 and the Atmos" conflates two things of opposite nature. Channel 14 is a control channel, read only by the processor. The real Atmos/PCM choice in the cinema is a side effect of whether that signal arrives intact: no AFM, no sample-rate conversion, no gain changes, and the correct UUID. That is why "reverted" fallbacks are silent, and why any re-wrap forces channel 14 to be regenerated — which DCP-o-matic already does automatically. On the software side, the limit is one of format, not technology. IAB is open and renderable (IABLib, easyDCP), while proprietary theatrical Atmos — especially encrypted — stays locked to Dolby hardware.

For DCP-o-matic, the reasonable investment is asymmetric. A channel selector in the player (and, in passing, fixing the "DBS" → "Sync" label on index 13) is a small piece of work on top of pieces that already exist. Playing back Atmos would only be defensible for unencrypted IAB, integrating IABLib, and even then it would first need validating that real legacy Atmos MXFs actually parse as Profile 1 — the central hypothesis no source confirms.
